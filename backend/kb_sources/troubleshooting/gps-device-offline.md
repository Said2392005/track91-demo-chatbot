## Overview

If a vehicle's GPS device shows as "offline" in Track91, the platform has stopped receiving
location pings from that device. This does not delete any history already recorded — it only
means no new location, speed, or health data is coming in until connectivity is restored.

## Common Causes

The most frequent causes of a device going offline are: the vehicle's ignition has been off for
an extended period and the device's backup battery has drained; the SIM card in the device has
lost network coverage or has an expired data plan; the device has come loose from its wiring
harness after maintenance work; or a firmware update is in progress and the device is
temporarily unreachable.

## Step-by-step Fix

First, confirm the vehicle has been driven or had its ignition on within the last few hours —
many devices only report while the vehicle is powered. Second, check the SIM card's data plan
status with your carrier; an expired or suspended SIM is the single most common cause of
prolonged offline periods. Third, physically inspect the device's wiring connection at the
OBD-II port or hardwired harness, and reseat it if loose. Fourth, if the device has a visible
LED indicator, confirm it is blinking in its normal pattern; a solid or unlit LED usually
indicates a power issue rather than a network issue.

## When to Contact Support

If a device has been offline for more than 24 hours after confirming the vehicle has been
driven, the SIM is active, and the wiring is secure, contact Track91 support with the vehicle's
plate number and the approximate time the device went offline. Support can check the device's
last-known diagnostic ping remotely before dispatching a technician.
